**Spatial Omics** technologies represent a major conceptual and methodological breakthrough in biomedical research, enabling the interrogation of gene expression, protein abundance, and lipid species while preserving tissue architecture and cellular native spatial coordinates.

In the liver, where metabolic functions are strictly coordinated along the porto-central axis (**hepatic zonation**), spatial omics are paramount for deciphering the molecular mechanisms underlying steatohepatitis (MASLD/MASH), progressive fibrosis, and hepatobiliary carcinogenesis.

#### Scientific Axes and Objectives:
- **Metabolic Zonation and Spatial Lipotoxicity:** Characterization of gradient accumulation patterns for triglycerides, ceramides, and phospholipid species across periportal and pericentral hepatocytes using Matrix-Assisted Laser Desorption/Ionization Mass Spectrometry Imaging (MALDI-MSI).
- **Tumor Microenvironment and Pre-metastatic Niche:** Spatial transcriptomics mapping in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) and cholangiocarcinoma (CCA) specimens to investigate intra-tumor heterogeneity, immune cell infiltration, and metabolically reprogrammed cellular niches.
- **Computational Multi-Omic Integration:** Bioinformatic integration of single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq), spatial transcriptomics, and quantitative lipidomic profiles conducted in close collaboration with the UPV/EHU SGIker Central Services and IIS Biocruces Bizkaia.
