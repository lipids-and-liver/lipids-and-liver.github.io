Metabolic dysfunction–associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) is the most common cause of chronic liver disease, affecting over 30% of adults. It can progress from simple steatosis to metabolic dysfunction–associated steatohepatitis (MASH), fibrosis, cirrhosis, and, in some cases, hepatocellular carcinoma. Cholesterol accumulation is a key driver of MASLD, as well as gallstone formation and cardiovascular disease. The first part of this work demonstrates that the transcription factor E2F2 regulates biliary metabolism in MASH via miR-34a-5p. Preclinical models show that E2f2 deficiency protects against liver lipid and bile acid accumulation by increasing bile acid and biliary lipid synthesis and secretion, an effect mediated through miR-34a-5p. Liver samples from individuals with advanced MASLD and dyslipidemia exhibit increased E2F2 and miR-34a-5p expression, supporting a role for this axis in biliary metabolism. Additionally, E2F2 contributes to cholestatic liver injury, and its deficiency protects against bile acid–induced liver damage. The second part investigates the role of E2F2 in lipoprotein metabolism and its interaction with PCSK9, a key regulator of circulating cholesterol, highlighting the contribution of this axis to dyslipidemia and cardiovascular disease. The role of PCSK9 in liver lipid accumulation is also addressed, supporting the involvement of the E2F2–PCSK9 axis in the metabolic dysregulation underlying MASLD progression. Overall, these findings provide novel insights into E2F2 and its downstream targets in cholesterol metabolism during MASLD progression and underscore their potential as therapeutic targets.
